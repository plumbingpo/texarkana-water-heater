Water around a water heater is one of the most common calls we get, and the first question is always the same: where is it actually coming from? It might be the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge line, the drain valve near the bottom of the tank, the inlet and outlet fittings on top, condensation on a cold tank, or a nearby pipe that has nothing to do with the heater. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Moisture or rust flakes under the burner access cover
  • Water dripping from the relief valve or out of the end of its discharge pipe
  • Rust, green corrosion, or white crust on the fittings at the top of the tank
  • Water stains, soft flooring, or musty smells near the heater closet
  • Higher water use with no obvious explanation
  • A puddle or damp spot on the floor around the base of the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water paired with dampness at the base
  • Steady drips from the drain valve near the base

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Leaks look simple and often are not. Water can start at a top fitting, run down under the jacket, and show up at the base, and condensation on cold tanks add confusion. The risks go beyond a wrong part. Plugging a dripping relief valve removes the protection against overheating and overpressure, forcing a corroded connection can crack it wide open, and electric heaters bring high-voltage wiring into a wet area. And if the tank itself is failing, no DIY fix will hold for long.

The value of a professional is a confirmed answer. You get the true source, the right parts, and an explanation of why it failed. When a relief valve drips, we test pressure and expansion. When a fitting is rotten, we replace the connection rather than tightening it harder. When condensation is the only issue, we explain it and save you a repair. And when the tank itself is leaking, we recommend replacement honestly, with a drain pan, drain line, and leak alarm to limit damage if it ever happens again.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Make a habit of checking around the heater for moisture or corrosion every few weeks
  • Install a drain pan piped to a safe spot, which matters most for attic, closet, and upstairs heaters
  • Annual flushing removes sediment that traps heat against the tank floor
  • Ask about expansion control if your home has a check valve or backflow preventer
  • High street pressure is hard on water heaters, so a pressure test and regulator can prevent repeat drips
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years; once it is used up, the tank starts to corrode
  • Put a water leak sensor on the floor next to the tank so a drip gets noticed early
