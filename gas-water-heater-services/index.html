Most gas water heaters in Texarkana, TX work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If you ever smell gas, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Texarkana technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to

Start with the hazards. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
