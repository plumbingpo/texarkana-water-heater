Kitchen Hot Water

Food-Service Hot Water Systems Built for the Rush

Dish machines, sanitizing sinks, and handwashing stations all compete for the same hot water, and an undersized or failing heater reveals itself as lukewarm water at the dish machine and stacks of dirty plates. A high-temperature dish machine usually needs final rinse water near 180°F, which almost always calls for a booster heater paired with the main system. We handle high-input tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters, and sizing for peak demand, and we schedule every visit around your prep and service times.

Hot water temperature is a critical concern for any food-service operator. Health inspectors check water temperature at handwashing sinks and sanitizing stations, and a dish machine that can't reach its rinse temperature can stop service. Our work keeps your system delivering the temperatures and volume your operation depends on, with documented service records that show a well-maintained facility to inspectors and landlords. You get detailed reports after each visit, along with the documentation many commercial leases require.

Equipment replacements and maintenance schedules are planned alongside owners, managers, and kitchen staff, so everything fits your operating schedule. If a job can't be done during service, we do it after close, so hot water is ready when the first shift arrives.

Safe Installation

Safe Installations, Documented Service

Commercial property owners have a duty of care to provide safe conditions for employees, customers, and tenants. A poorly installed or neglected water heater can result in carbon monoxide hazards from bad venting, scalding at the tap, bacterial growth in tanks kept too cool, and costly water damage from a failed tank. Texarkana Water Heater provides commercial installations and inspections that cover venting and combustion air, gas line sizing, relief valve discharge piping, expansion tanks, thermostatic mixing valves, drain pans, seismic strapping where required, and permits and inspections. Our written reports identify risks, assign priority levels, and recommend specific preventive actions.

This work serves a dual purpose: it protects people and property, and it provides the documentation that demonstrates due diligence to landlords, insurers, and health or building inspectors. We maintain detailed service logs, temperature records, relief valve test dates, and a documentation binder for every account, with more frequent service for heavy-use equipment in kitchens, laundries, and large mechanical rooms.
