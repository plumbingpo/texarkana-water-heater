Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Hooks

Most premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Hooks Homeowners Face

Water heaters in and around Hooks deal with a familiar set of stresses. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Then there's age. When the sacrificial anode rod wears out, often well before the tank itself looks old, the steel begins corroding from within, and a slow leak at the base often shows up next. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater often means updating the installation along the way: an expansion tank if the water system is closed, a T&P discharge line piped to an approved spot, venting that drafts properly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Well water brings its own challenges, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes shows up when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most cost far less to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (903) 202-2374 and we'll take a look.
