A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater

  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting

  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A new water heater installation is really several trades in one job. The gas piping must be sized for the heater plus every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested until it is leak-free. The vent must meet the manufacturer's rules on diameter, length, pitch, and clearances, or carbon monoxide can find its way indoors. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. The gas line, vent, and circuit are planned before the first cut, the permit is handled when required, and the relief valve discharge and code items go in correctly. Every joint is tested, every gas install is checked for draft and carbon monoxide, and the temperature is set to protect against scalding. Hurried installs leave behind undersized gas lines, poor vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
