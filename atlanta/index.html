How Atlanta Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Most water heater problems we see in Atlanta come back to what's in the water. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Well water may bring sediment, iron, or sulfur into the tank, and a sulfur smell in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a swap to a different anode type can solve. Older homes often need code upgrades when a heater is replaced: an expansion tank on closed systems, a properly routed T&P discharge line, a drain pan where a leak could damage finished space, and seismic straps in areas that require them. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early.
