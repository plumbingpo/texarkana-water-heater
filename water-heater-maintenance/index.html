The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Texarkana, TX, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

A Texarkana Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Maintenance

Some upkeep is fair game for homeowners: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes serious torque on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

Professional maintenance also brings the right parts on the truck, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Experience counts as much as equipment: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
