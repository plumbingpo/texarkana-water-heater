No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Texarkana Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly corrodes away, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection right away. Catching it early often keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always less expensive and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Texarkana Homes

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our annual maintenance. On each scheduled visit, we flush the sediment out of the tank, check the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all matched to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat more efficiently, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Most repair calls we take come down to a handful of recurring problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The hot water runs out fast, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
